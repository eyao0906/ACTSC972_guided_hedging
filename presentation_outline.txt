FOUR MINUTES, THREE SLIDES, THEN ONE SHORT QUESTION

Slide 1 - Question and prediction (about 45 seconds)
State the chosen experiment, baseline, changed parameter(s), and prediction.
Say which quantities remain fixed and whether retraining is required.

Slide 2 - Evidence (about 90 seconds)
Show one clearly labelled figure and, if helpful, a compact table.
State the test sample size and identify the strategies and benchmarks compared.
Architecture option: include parameter counts, selected updates, and training time.
Student-designed option: explain the chosen comparison and any additional training.

Slide 3 - Interpretation and limits (about 105 seconds)
Give the main finding, a possible explanation, and one limitation.
Relate the result to costs, information, or the risk objective as appropriate.
An inconclusive result is acceptable; do not claim more than one run establishes.

Possible follow-up questions
- Why does an early holding not depend on future prices?
- What changes if previous holdings are detached from the computation graph?
- Why is eta one scalar, and why is test ES recalculated from test losses?
- What happens to turnover when only the charged cost rate changes?
- Which evidence would be needed before claiming one architecture is better?
